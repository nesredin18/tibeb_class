1.1 What is Physics?

Physics is the study of the fundamental constituents of the universe and the laws that govern their behavior. It encompasses a wide range of phenomena, from the motion of celestial bodies to the behavior of subatomic particles. Physics seeks to understand the underlying principles that govern the natural world through observation, experimentation, and mathematical modeling.

1.2 Branches of Physics

Physics can be broadly divided into several branches, each focusing on a specific aspect of the physical world. Some of the major branches include:

  • Mechanics: The study of motion and forces.
  • Thermodynamics: The study of heat and energy.
  • Electromagnetism: The study of electricity and magnetism.
  • Optics: The study of light and its behavior.
  • Quantum Mechanics: The study of the behavior of matter at the atomic and subatomic levels.
  • Relativity: The study of space, time, and gravity.

1.3 The Scientific Method

The scientific method is a systematic approach to investigating the natural world. It typically involves the following steps:

  1. Observation: Observing a phenomenon or identifying a problem.
  2. Hypothesis: Formulating a testable explanation for the observation.
  3. Experimentation: Designing and conducting experiments to test the hypothesis.
  4. Analysis: Analyzing the experimental data to determine whether it supports or refutes the hypothesis.
  5. Conclusion: Drawing conclusions based on the analysis and refining the hypothesis if necessary.

1.4 Measurement and Units

Measurement is the process of assigning a numerical value to a physical quantity. In physics, it is essential to use standardized units to ensure consistency and comparability of measurements. The International System of Units (SI) is the most widely used system of units in science and engineering. Some common SI units include:

  • Meter (m): Unit of length.
  • Kilogram (kg): Unit of mass.
  • Second (s): Unit of time.
  • Ampere (A): Unit of electric current.
  • Kelvin (K): Unit of temperature.
  • Mole (mol): Unit of amount of substance.
  • Candela (cd): Unit of luminous intensity.
